# Plotline Notes, issue 12: Why I almost quit book two

I wrote my first novel with no outline. It took four years. Book two, I swore, would be different.

It wasn't, at first. Three months in I had 40,000 words and no idea what happened next. I nearly quit.

What saved it was a dumb trick: I wrote every scene on an index card and laid them on the floor. Act one by the door, act two in the middle, act three by the window. The gaps were obvious. The middle had no turn.

So I wrote one card: "she chooses the job over her sister." Everything after it fell into place.

Three things I learned:
1. Outline after a messy draft, not only before.
2. Look for the middle turn first. If act two has no choice in it, it will sag.
3. Physical cards beat a list, because you can see the gaps.

That floor is why Plotline has lanes. And this month we shipped export to Word and Markdown, so your outline can leave the app.

My writing group friend Rosa Kim said it best: "An outline is a map you're allowed to redraw."
